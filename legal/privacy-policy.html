Privacy Policy — Kids Story Time

Effective date: 29 August 2026 Last updated: 29 August 2026

This is the privacy policy for Kids Story Time, a 3D storybook app for children aged 3 and up, made by Reallexi LLC.

We wrote it to be read by a parent, not by a lawyer. It is short because the app does almost nothing with data — and everything below is a description of how the app actually works, not a list of things we promise to do one day.

1. The short version

  • No accounts. There is no sign-in. We never ask for a name, an email address, a birthday, a photo or a profile.
  • No ads. No advertising of any kind, and no advertising software inside the app.
  • No tracking, no analytics. We do not measure what your child reads, we do not build a profile, and the app never touches your device's advertising identifier (IDFA on iPhone and iPad, Advertising ID on Android).
  • It works offline. All twenty stories are inside the app when you download it. The app does not need the internet to read a story.
  • One narrow exception. If a grown-up buys a membership, the app sends the store's purchase receipt and a random install number to our server so the membership can be unlocked. That is the only time the app sends anything anywhere. Section 4 explains it in full.
  • Everything else stays on the device. Reading progress and settings are saved on the device only. They never reach us.

3. What we collect from your child: nothing

We do not collect personal information from children, and we do not collect it from grown-ups either, apart from the purchase exception in section 4.

Specifically, the app never collects, asks for, or has access to:

  • names, nicknames, email addresses, phone numbers or postal addresses
  • birthdays, ages or age ranges
  • photos, videos or anything from the camera — the app does not use the camera
  • voice recordings or anything from the microphone — the app does not use the microphone
  • contacts, calendars, files or your photo library
  • location of any kind, precise or approximate
  • your device's advertising identifier (IDFA / Advertising ID)
  • persistent identifiers used to recognise you across other apps or websites

There is no chat, no comments, no sharing, no user-uploaded content, no messaging and no way for your child to reach another person through this app.

There are no third-party software kits inside the app for advertising, analytics, attribution, crash reporting or social networks. There is nothing in the app that reports back about your child.

4. The one exception: buying a membership

Two stories are free forever. The other eighteen are opened by the Story Time Membership ($3.99 per month or $42.99 per year). Buying one is the only thing that sends data off the device.

Here is exactly what happens, step by step.

a. The install number. The first time the app runs, it makes up a random number for itself — a UUID, something like 7f3a9c1e-…. It is generated on the device, by the device. It is not derived from anything about you or your phone, it is not your device's identifier, and it is not linked to any person. We call it the install id. It sits on the device with the other saved settings.

b. The purchase itself. The purchase is made by Apple or Google, in their own payment sheet, using the payment method already on your store account. We never see, receive or store card numbers, bank details, billing addresses or your Apple ID / Google account. We are not told who bought the membership.

c. Checking the purchase. After the store confirms the purchase, the app sends two things to our server at api-kidsstorytime.reallexi.io:

  1. the store's purchase receipt — a signed Apple StoreKit token, or a Google Play purchase token; and
  2. the install id from step (a).

d. What our server does. Our server asks Apple or Google whether that receipt is genuine and still valid. If it is, the server saves one purchase record containing:

  • which store it came from (Apple or Google),
  • the store's transaction identifiers for that purchase,
  • which product was bought (monthly or yearly membership),
  • when the paid period ends, and
  • the install ids that have used that purchase (so the same membership works on the family's other devices), with the dates the record was created and last updated.

The server also keeps the receipt itself, so it can re-check the purchase with Apple or Google later — for example when the subscription renews.

The server then sends back a small signed membership pass, which the app stores on the device. The pass is what unlocks the stories, and the app can check it by itself with no internet at all — which is why a paid membership keeps working on a plane or in a car with no signal.

e. What is not in any of that. No name. No email. No age. No address. No reading history. The purchase record cannot tell us who you are; it identifies a store transaction and a random install number, and nothing else. If you and your neighbour both bought a membership, we could not tell which record was which.

When the app talks to our server at all: only for this. If nobody has ever bought a membership, the app never sends anything to us. If someone has, the app re-checks the stored receipt when it starts and has a connection, so that renewals and cancellations are picked up. No other message is ever sent.

f. A note about the web version. Everything above describes the installed app on iPhone, iPad and Android, and it is equally true of the version that runs in a browser at kidsstorytime.reallexi.io. The one difference is unavoidable and applies to every website in the world: to send you the page, our server receives the ordinary request your browser makes, which includes your IP address and the kind of browser you are using. We do not use that to identify anyone, we do not build profiles from it, we do not share it, and there are no tracking or advertising cookies on the site. Once the page has loaded, reading works offline exactly as it does in the app.

Our own server keeps no access logs at all — it records no addresses, no pages and no times. The network provider in front of it (Cloudflare) keeps short-lived operational records of its own to route and protect the connection, which we do not receive and cannot search.

5. What is saved on the device, and stays there

The app remembers a few things so it feels friendly the next time it is opened. All of this lives in the app's own storage on the device (browser local storage on the web; the app's storage on iPhone, iPad and Android). None of it is ever sent to us or to anyone else.

Saved on the device What it is for
Reading progress Reopening a story on the page your child stopped at
Reading mode — living book or paper book Remembering the mode your child prefers
Read-aloud narration on/off Remembering the narration choice
Music and sounds on/off Remembering the sound choice
Accessibility settings — text size, dark or light, contrast, reduced motion, captions, audio descriptions Keeping the app set up the way it suits your child
Graphics quality Keeping the picture smooth on your device
The install id, the membership pass and the store receipt (section 4) Unlocking the membership offline

There are no tracking cookies and no advertising cookies. Nothing here follows anyone around the internet.

Deleting all of it is simple: delete the app (or clear the site data in your browser). Everything in the table above goes with it, immediately and completely, because it was only ever on the device. If you had a membership, tap Restore purchases after reinstalling and it comes back — you are not charged again.

6. Reading aloud, and the sounds

The read-aloud narration uses the voice already built into your device (the same one used by your phone's accessibility features). The story text is spoken by the device. Nothing is recorded, nothing is sent to a voice service, and the microphone is never used or requested.

The music and sound effects are generated by the app itself while it plays. No sound files are downloaded and nothing is streamed.

7. Children's privacy — COPPA and UK/EU GDPR-K

Kids Story Time is made for children aged 3 and up, so children's privacy rules apply to it in full. We follow them the simplest way possible: by not collecting personal information at all.

United States — COPPA (the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act). We do not collect, use or disclose personal information from children under 13. Because we collect none, there is nothing for a parent to consent to, nothing for us to disclose, and nothing for us to hand over or delete on request. We do not condition your child's use of any part of the app on giving us personal information, because we never ask for any. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone of any age beyond the purchase record described in section 4, which is not personal information about a child and is created only by a grown-up who has passed the parental gate.

United Kingdom and European Union — the GDPR, including children's data (often called GDPR-K) and the UK Age Appropriate Design Code. We do not process personal data about children. There is no profiling of any kind. There is no behavioural or personalised advertising — there is no advertising at all. There are no "nudge" techniques pushing a child towards weaker privacy settings or more time in the app; the privacy-protective setting is the only setting. The app's defaults are the safest ones, and nothing about a child's use of the app is shared with anyone.

No behavioural advertising, ever. We do not show ads, we do not sell or share data for advertising, and we do not use anyone's information — child or adult — to target advertising, build audiences or profile anybody. There is nothing in this app that could do so.

Purchases are behind a parental gate. Before any purchase button appears, the app asks a multiplication question that a young child cannot answer. The payment itself then happens in Apple's or Google's own sheet, which asks for your store password, Face ID or fingerprint. A child cannot buy anything on their own.

8. Who else sees anything

We do not sell data. We do not share data for advertising. We do not have advertising partners, analytics partners, data brokers or affiliates receiving anything at all.

The only companies involved are:

Apple and Google — as the shops that take the payment. When you buy a membership, Apple (App Store / StoreKit) or Google (Google Play Billing) handles the payment and holds the payment details. They are the merchant, not us. We receive their receipt and ask them to confirm it is genuine. Their handling of your purchase is covered by their own privacy policies:

  • Apple: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/
  • Google: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Our own hosting. Our purchase server runs on infrastructure we operate, reached through a content-delivery network that passes requests through to it. Those providers process the connection itself; they are not given, and do not receive, anything about your child.

In practice that means one thing: our purchase server runs on a virtual private server we rent and administer ourselves, and it is reached through Cloudflare, which carries the connection and protects it. Cloudflare handles the connection; it is not given, and does not receive, anything about your child.

We would only disclose a purchase record if the law required it — for example a valid court order — or to defend against fraud against us. Since the records identify a store transaction and a random number, there is very little in them to disclose.

10. Your rights, and how to use them

Depending on where you live, you have rights over personal data about you — to see it, correct it, delete it, restrict how it is used, or complain to a regulator. In the UK and EU these come from the GDPR; in California from the CCPA/CPRA; other places have similar rules.

Because there are no accounts, here is honestly what we can and cannot do:

What we can do

  • Tell you everything a purchase record holds — section 4(d) is the complete list, and we can send you the actual record.
  • Delete a purchase record, unless we still have to keep it for tax or accounting. If we delete it, the membership bought with it can no longer be restored on a new device.
  • Answer any question about how the app works, and correct anything wrong in a record.
  • Stop using a record for anything except the legal duty to keep it.

What we cannot do

  • We cannot look you up by name or email, because we have never held either. To find a record we need something from the purchase itself: the order or transaction id on the receipt Apple or Google emailed you, or the store you bought from and the approximate date.
  • We cannot delete your reading progress or settings for you, because we do not have them. Deleting the app removes them, instantly and entirely.
  • We cannot refund you — Apple and Google take the payment and issue refunds. See the Refund policy.

How to ask. Email admin@reallexi.io with what you would like. We aim to reply within a few days, and always within one month. We do not charge for this, and we will not ask you to create an account in order to exercise a right.

Complaining. If you are in the UK or EU and think we have handled data badly, you may complain to your national data protection authority (in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office, ico.org.uk). We would rather you told us first so we can put it right.

Legal basis (UK/EU). For the purchase record we rely on performance of a contract — you bought a membership and we have to unlock it — and, for keeping it afterwards, our legal obligation to retain sales records. We do not rely on consent for anything, because we do not do anything that would need it.

11. How we keep it safe

  • The app talks to our server only over an encrypted connection (HTTPS).
  • Every receipt is verified directly with Apple or Google before anything is unlocked. A made-up or replayed receipt is rejected.
  • The membership pass is signed by our server with a private key that never leaves it. The app can only check passes, never create them.
  • Each purchase produces exactly one record, tied to one store transaction — so a receipt cannot be reused to conjure extra memberships.
  • Our server holds no names, emails or passwords, because it was never given any. There is no login to break into and no customer database to steal.
  • Access to the server is limited to the people who run it, and it holds nothing about children.

No system is perfect, and we will not pretend otherwise. But the strongest protection here is the design: information we never collect cannot leak.
